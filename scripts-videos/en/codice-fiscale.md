> How to get your codice fiscale
> The codice fiscale is your personal number in Italy (16 letters and digits). You need it to work, rent, see a doctor and open an account.
> It is free and does not expire. Nobody should charge you for it.

## Escena 2

**Imagen:** El formulario AA4/8 con cada casilla resaltada

**Voz:**

> Write in CAPITAL LETTERS and exactly as in your passport. Bring your original passport on the day.
> Tick “Attribuzione del codice fiscale” if it is your first time. The other options are for changes or duplicates.
> Your surname(s), exactly as in your passport.
> Your first name(s), exactly as in your passport.
> M (male) or F (female).
> Day, month and year: for example 05/09/1990.
> If you were born outside Italy, write your country's name in Italian (for example PERÙ, MAROCCO, UCRAINA). Leave the province empty.
> Your address in Italy: street and number, CAP (postcode), municipality (comune) and province.
> Only if you do not live in Italy yet: your address abroad (country, city and street).
> Only for minors or if someone else signs for you (parent or guardian): their details.
> Date and your signature. You can sign in front of the clerk on the day.

## Escena 3

**Imagen:** Oficina de la Agenzia delle Entrate (foto de oficinas)

**Voz:**

> Where: at any Agenzia delle Entrate office (best with an appointment). If you enter on a work or family-reunion visa, the Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione often assigns it; abroad, the Italian consulate.
> What to bring: a valid passport, the completed AA4/8 and, if you have them, your residence permit, visa or receipt.
> You get a certificate with the code the same day. If you register with the public health service, the health card arrives later by post and also works as your codice fiscale.
